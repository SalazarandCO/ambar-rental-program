01 — The Strategy in One Idea

Sell the brand and the hands-off income — then follow the people who already come, buy, and can fly in.

The product is the message: a furnished residence five miles from Walt Disney World, professionally managed by Marriott, earning rental income while the owner keeps up to 30 days of personal use. The data then dictates where to spend — only the cities where three independent signals overlap: (1) proven Florida purchase intent, (2) heavy Orlando tourist flow, and (3) nonstop MCO flights.

Where we are today — and what comes next

AMBAR's paid engine is already running in Brazil, driven by a dedicated Brazil marketing partner — confirmed live in the Meta Ad Library, where the active "Ambar Orlando" creative is Portuguese.[7] That's Phase 1, and it's working. This plan is about what to layer on next: activating the other markets the data identifies as highest-intent — Colombia, Mexico, Canada, the UK, and U.S. Hispanic — which AMBAR's own channels have not yet reached. Brazil keeps running in parallel; everything here is additive.

04 — The Two Audiences

One project, two very different funnels.

Buyers and brokers need different objectives, creative, platforms, and KPIs. Why separate them: a shared campaign forces one optimization target and lets the cheaper action (e.g. broker sign-ups) starve the higher-value one (buyer leads). Splitting them protects budget and learning on each.

Audience A · International Investors

Demand generation → qualified lead

Who: Affluent, largely cash-ready adults 35–65 — investors seeking hands-off rental income (Latin America) and second-/vacation-home buyers (Canada, UK) who value brand names.

The hook: the Apartments by Marriott Bonvoy rental program — a furnished residence near Disney that Marriott operates and markets, generating income with no landlord work, plus Bonvoy owner perks and 30 personal nights/year. Sell trust, turnkey income, and a globally recognized brand.

Channels

  • Meta (Advantage+ / prospecting): the demand engine abroad (see §6 — search is thin there). Lookalikes off inquiry & WhatsApp leads.
  • Meta retargeting: landing-page visitors, video viewers, IG engagers.
  • Google Search: intent capture — strongest in the U.S. (EN + ES) and on brand terms.
  • YouTube / PMax: visual brand + cross-channel retargeting.

Lead event: inquiry / brochure request / WhatsApp. KPI: cost per qualified lead → cost per reservation.

Audience B · Brokers & Realtors

Referral-channel recruitment

Who: Bilingual agents & brokerages who already own the buyer relationships — Bogotá, CDMX, Toronto, Miami, San Juan. The live audit proves this channel is already active (and currently shared with competitors).[7]

The hook: sell a Marriott-branded product clients trust, plus a co-broke commission and a done-for-you kit (deck, renders, brand assets, WhatsApp scripts).

Channels

  • LinkedIn: precise job-title targeting (Realtor, Broker, Agent).
  • Meta: real-estate interest/behavior audiences; cheap retargeting.
  • Google Search: "Orlando new construction referral," "programa de referidos Orlando."

KPI: cost per registered broker → buyers sourced per broker.

Meta restricts granular targeting on housing ads (Special Ad Category) for the buyer campaigns — we work within it via geography + broad creative + lookalikes; LinkedIn carries precise role targeting for brokers.

05 — The Budget · every split justified

What to spend, where it goes, and why.

This budget funds the expansion markets (Colombia, Mexico, Canada, UK, U.S. Hispanic + brokers). Brazil continues in parallel through your Brazil partner. Figures are monthly media; add ~15–20% for management + creative.

What's grounded vs. recommended

The budget below is a recommended plan, not a measured spend. Externally grounded: the Google CPCs (live Keyword Planner, §6), Meta's documented learning-phase threshold, and published real-estate cost-per-lead benchmarks. The tier size, line-item splits and lead/cost targets are planning assumptions — refined against AMBAR's own actuals once campaigns run.

Why $15,000 as the recommended tier

Meta's learning phase needs roughly 50 optimization events per ad set per week to stabilize and optimize reliably (Meta Business Help Center) — so spend must be concentrated enough per market to reach that, not spread thin across many geos. Sizing the rest is a planning assumption: at a blended ~$45–55 per qualified lead (the midpoint band of the §9 targets) — deliberately above the ~$17–28 raw real-estate Facebook lead-ad benchmark (WordStream / LocaliQ 2024–25) because an AMBAR lead is a vetted $375K cross-border buyer, not a form-fill — $15K/mo implies ~270–330 qualified leads/mo, enough for most ad sets to optimize while covering the market map. Treat $15K as a recommended starting tier, not a derived requirement: $8K tests 2–3 markets, $30K scales once cost-per-reservation is proven.

Recommended allocation — two phases

Why this changed — reweighting toward brokers

The companion Sales & Distribution Playbook shows brokers drive 60–75% of units, yet a single buyer-heavy plan pointed only ~16% of media at brokers. So the plan is now phased. The nuance — and why we don't simply move all the money to broker ads: ad dollars don't sign brokers; business development, the commission/allocation offer, the deal-registration portal and broker events do (NAR: 72% of foreign-buyer leads come through referrals/relationships, only 15% online). Media's broker job is narrow but real — recruit agents at scale, fill broker/investor events, and manufacture buyer leads to route to brokers as ammunition. Buyer ads aren't "retail vs. broker": the demand they create is what makes the broker channel worth joining.

Phase 1 — Launch (~first 90 days): build the broker channel

Tilted to recruiting brokers and filling events, with enough buyer demand to seed a starter lead pool brokers can sell against. Hold here until the master-broker network is signed and the portal is live. Rationale: this is the window where the 60–75% channel gets built; broker + event spend rises to ~40%.

Why the Meta / Google split is 58 / 42 (buyer budget)

META 58%
GOOGLE 42%

This split is derived from the live data, not a rule of thumb:

  • Google spend is capped by existing search volume — and the live pull shows that volume is limited.[8] International in-country purchase terms run just 10–100 searches/mo (Colombia, UK on US-style terms); even U.S. head terms top out at 1K–10K/mo. You cannot deploy large Search budget against queries that don't exist. Search is intent capture — it can only buy demand that's already there.
  • Meta is demand generation — uncapped by search volume. A pre-construction branded residence is a discovery purchase: international buyers aren't typing "pre-construction condo Orlando," they have to be shown it. Meta reaches a live-confirmed 6–23M real-estate-investing adults per international expansion market (see §7) regardless of search behavior, and is the only meaningful lever in the thin-search international geos.
  • The buyer journey is top-funnel-led: 93% of international buyers visit Florida before purchasing[4] — inspiration and consideration (Meta video/lifestyle) precede any search. AMBAR's active program already leans almost entirely on Meta — its own creative claims "80% sold in 90 days."[7]
  • Why Google still earns a strong 42% (not less): U.S. Hispanic + English search volume is genuinely large and growing ("apartamentos en orlando florida" 10K–100K/mo; "casas en venta en orlando" +900% in 3 months) and clicks are cheap (mostly $1–5 top-of-page).[8] That high-intent, low-cost capture — plus brand-defense search (people who saw the Meta ad then Google "Ambar Orlando") — is too efficient to under-fund.

The 58/42 ratio itself is a starting judgment, not a measured optimum — anchored to the live search data above (thin international search caps how much Google can absorb) and to the general industry pattern that new-development lead-gen leans social. Google holds a robust 42% because the live Keyword Planner data shows real, cheap U.S. search demand. The phase tables above run slightly more Meta-heavy than 58/42 — the Phase 2 buyer lines sit near 61/39 — because early demand-gen leans social; the mix converges toward 58/42 as cheap U.S. Search scales. We rebalance monthly toward whichever channel delivers the lower cost-per-reservation.

Why the audience & geo splits

Steady-state (Phase 2) split — Buyers 77% / Brokers 16% / Reserve 7%: buyers are the revenue event; brokers are high-leverage (one broker → many buyers) but, in media terms, a recruit-at-scale spend rather than where most broker sign-ups actually originate. In Phase 1 this flips to ~40% broker recruitment + events while the channel is being built (rationale above). Geo (within buyers): Tier-1 international ~60% / U.S. Hispanic ~35% / reserve ~5% — international skews higher because intent is strongest there and AMBAR isn't yet present, while U.S. Hispanic earns a third for being the cheapest, highest-volume, fastest-closing pool (§6). Brazil sits outside this budget, with the partner.

A broker shopping for a client types the same high-intent terms a serious investor does — there is no separate "broker" search to buy. What this means for the broker channel

There is no budget-worthy standalone "broker" search market — and Meta has no precise real-estate-agent audience either. So we don't build a separate broker campaign on either platform; we cover the income/STR/condo-hotel cluster above (which catches brokers and investors in one motion), and we recruit brokers as people — via LinkedIn, BD outreach and events (§5, and the Sales & Distribution Playbook). Rationale: on Google, broker demand shows up as client-need capture, not recruitment; the recruitment lever is human, not media.
