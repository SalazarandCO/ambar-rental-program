01 — The Model

Two layers, one anchor, total price discipline.

Run distribution as a two-layer channel: (1) a master broker who owns the international mandate, recruits sub-brokers, and runs marketing; (2) a co-broke network of in-country agents who bring buyers and are paid through a Florida-licensed structure. The whole thing only works on top of a deal-registration system + one fixed price list — without that, the resellers already posting AMBAR will keep undercutting you.

The single highest-leverage signing

PFS Realty Group is the one contract that covers four feeder markets at once — verified offices in Bogotá, Medellín, Cali, and Mexico City plus Miami, 65+ advisors across 8 countries, 2,000+ Latin American families served, and a business model already built on selling sub-$500K Florida investment property with financing + rental management attached. Their buyer is AMBAR's buyer. Call them first. (Founders Gustavo Gálvez & Patricia Gómez · pfsrealty.com)

02 — The Partnership Structure (this is what sells brokers)

Make it worth their time, then make it impossible to cheat.

Brokers choose what to push based on commission, ease, and trust that they'll get paid. At 7/322 sold and off the Miami luxury strip, AMBAR competes for broker mindshare — so pay at the top of the band and remove every reason for a broker to hesitate.

The commission stack — budget ~8–10% total selling cost

LayerRateWhy
Co-broke to the selling broker6%Miami new-dev norm is 3–6% (5% standard); soft market is pushing to 7%. Pay the top to win attention for a lower-price, must-sell product.
Master-broker override1–2%Their margin for running the program, recruiting, marketing, training. Standard mechanism that makes a mandate worthwhile.
Velocity / tier bonus pool0.5–1%Accelerators (+0.5% after a broker's 3rd unit, +1% after 5th), launch-window cash bonuses, top-seller prize.

Master-broker mandate — model term sheet

  • Grant & exclusivity: exclusive international/LatAm sales mandate for the 322 units (developer keeps US-domestic + onsite). Exclusivity is their incentive to invest in marketing.
  • Term: 12 months, auto-renew in 6-month increments only on hitting a sales-pace hurdle (e.g., X units/quarter). Short leash on a velocity product.
  • Compensation & timing: developer pays co-broke, master administers; commission earned on the hard, non-refundable contract, part-paid then, balance at closing; clawback if a deal fails.
  • Marketing co-op: developer-funded budget the master spends to an approved plan (keeps brand + Marriott "Apartments by Bonvoy" usage compliant).
  • Unit release & rising tiers: release inventory in allocations/phases at stepped prices — first money in gets the lowest tier; brokers sell "buy before the next release."
  • Deal registration: only registered brokers earn commission; the buyer is credited to the first broker to register the named lead in the portal; 60–90 day protection window. This is the rule that forces freelancers to register and accept your terms.

Legal must-knows — don't skip

  • Foreign agents must co-broke through a FL-licensed broker. An overseas agent can be paid a referral/co-broke fee only if licensed in their country, performing no brokerage activity inside the US, disclosed to all parties. The master broker is the legal counterparty for every international agent.
  • RESPA §8: commission splits are legal only for actual brokerage work and must be disclosed; penalties $10K+ per violation. Keep all comp tied to real brokerage services and documented.
  • Post-NAR settlement: you can't broadcast co-broke via MLS the old way — put the co-broke offer in writing in the registration/co-broke agreement.
  • Escrow (FL §718.202): buyer deposits up to 10% held in escrow — a concrete trust point for pre-construction buyers; structure commission timing around it.

Commission benchmarks are sourced industry norms, not AMBAR-specific quotes — confirm in negotiation. Not legal advice; have FL counsel paper the mandate and registration system.

05 — Broker Launch & Incentive Playbook

How Miami developers actually win broker loyalty.

  • Sales-gallery launch + broker events in feeder cities. Open a branded Orlando sales gallery; run broker launch events in Bogotá, Mexico City, São Paulo and Buenos Aires alongside the master broker.
  • Broker caravans. Fly/host top international brokers to Orlando for a property + Disney-proximity tour with branded hospitality.
  • Lead with the 10% leaseback + Marriott management — it's at the top of the incentive range competitors offer (Grove, Legacy) and is the headline broker pitch.
  • Incentives: rising price tiers; commission accelerators (+0.5% after 3rd unit, +1% after 5th); launch-window cash bonus per unit; a Bonvoy top-seller trip as the annual prize (on-brand, low cash cost).

07 — Private Investor Dinners (London & Toronto)

Where there's no good expo, host the room yourself.

London and Toronto lack strong retail US-property expos, so intimate, high-touch dinners out-convert any booth. (A Dubai developer ran exactly this model at Park Hyatt Toronto — proof it works in-market.)

  • Format: weeknight, 2.5–3 hrs, private room at a five-star hotel (on-brand: a Marriott luxury property — St. Regis, JW Marriott). Cocktails → dinner → 10–12 min deck → numbers + Q&A → limited-allocation close with reservation desk.
  • Size: 20–40 pre-qualified guests, ranked by intent beforehand.
  • Source attendees via: the brokers you meet at REALTOR QUEST (Toronto) and APITS/Property Investor Show (London) — they bring their client books; plus wealth managers, EB-5/immigration attorneys, currency-transfer firms; and paid-social retargeting to drive gated RSVPs. Lead the invite with "Apartments by Marriott Bonvoy."
  • Budget: London £6–15K / Toronto CA$8–18K per dinner (room often free against an F&B minimum). Cluster dinners around the trade shows to share travel.
  • Expect: a handful of serious leads and 0–2 reservations per well-qualified 25-guest dinner — judge by pipeline, always pair with a follow-up sequence.
