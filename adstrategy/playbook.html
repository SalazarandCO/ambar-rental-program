01 — The Number That Governs Everything

Stop thinking "322." Think "the financing threshold."

Construction lenders fund only after a pre-sale threshold is met — historically ~70% of units under binding contract/deposit, now trending to ~50% depending on sponsor and market. That milestone, not full sell-out, is the real near-term target: it de-risks the project and unlocks the loan. Every channel below is pointed at it.

The strategic insight

Retail/DTC sales close one unit at a time. A bulk tranche (20–50 units in one deal) and a broker network with reserved allocations can move the threshold in months, not years. So sequence accordingly: pursue a bulk anchor deal and stand up the broker channel first; let events and ads feed both. Confirm your exact lender threshold and deadline — it sets the urgency for the bulk track.

Tier 2 — high-capacity master brokers (recruit for scale)

Pitch these as a volume/velocity product for their LatAm databases, not a luxury listing (AMBAR sits below their usual price band): ISG World / RelatedISG (master-broker mandate candidate, $12B+ sold), Fortune International (1,000+ multilingual agents), Cervera (pre-construction depth; runs Bogotá/CDMX/BA expos), ONE Sotheby's (development division + runs Canada/Brazil/Mexico seminars — also your best practical route into Canada), and the Brazil bench (Authentic USA, Vitória Realty, Casas em Miami) + 360 Agencia (Medellín).

The offer (commission & allocation)

  • Pay at the soft-market top end: 6–8% developer-paid to the registering outside broker. Miami developers are currently raising buy-side commissions to ~7% and cutting deposits to spur pre-sales — at 7/322 sold you compete for broker attention, so don't pay the 2–3% new-construction floor.
  • Master-broker override 1–2% for whoever runs a country's distribution (e.g., PFS for the Andean/Mexico flow, a Miami master like ISG for the LatAm-from-Miami flow).
  • Early-bird premium + volume bonus: highest commission and lowest price tier during the pre-sale push to the threshold, stepping down after. Add e.g. +1% after a broker's 5th unit.
  • Reserved unit allocations (best floors/views) to top brokers — the classic motivator; prevents double-selling the same unit.

The control layer — stand this up before you scale (fixes the reseller chaos)

  1. Deal-registration rule: only registered brokers earn commission, and the buyer is credited to the first broker to register the lead in the portal. This single rule forces freelancers to register (and accept terms) to get paid.
  2. One published price list + deposit schedule. No broker may advertise a different price, discount, or "guaranteed return" claim (leaseback ≠ guaranteed return beyond year 2 — a compliance trap).
  3. Controlled co-branded kit only: approved renders, floor plans, the leaseback/Bonvoy one-pager, fixed price sheet, EN/ES/PT versions — distributed only via the portal, version-controlled so old price sheets die. (Marriott "Apartments by Bonvoy" marks have usage rules — pre-clear assets.)
  4. "AMBAR Certified Sales Partner" training = portal access = right to use the marks. Covers the condo-hotel structure, leaseback→Bonvoy transition, deposit timeline, and FL foreign-buyer closing. Prefer brokers with the NAR CIPS designation.
  5. Convert or take down the unauthorized AMBAR pages already live (tdflorida, realtyinorlando, etc.): invite them to register under the program, or issue takedowns.

Tooling: run the channel on Property-xRM (developer broker portal + real-time inventory/allocation + deal registration), with Brokermint or BrokerSumo for commission payout automation.

04 — Channel 2 · Bulk / Institutional
Contingency lever · 0–10% base case

One tranche can de-risk the whole project.

Selling 20–50 units to a single buyer/group is the fastest way to clear the construction-loan pre-sale covenant. The key is targeting the right bulk buyer: yield/leaseback investors who pay a modest tranche discount — not distress "vultures" who want 40–50% off.

Your exact comp already happened in Orlando

The Grove Resort (Orlando, 878-unit condo-hotel, 8–10% guaranteed leaseback, ~$300K units) was sold out ahead of schedule by BTI Partners, partly to yield/1031 investors buying in blocks. That proves AMBAR's buyer pool exists at your price and structure. Lead bulk conversations with this comp.

Deal structure & economics

  • Tranche discount: target ~8–15% off the current retail tier (justified by removing 20–50 units of sales/marketing cost). Resist the distress anchor — "Condo Vultures"-type buyers want up to 50% off; that's the wrong buyer and it poisons retail pricing.
  • Demand hard, non-refundable deposits on the tranche so they count toward the construction-loan pre-sale covenant — that is the entire strategic point of bulk.
  • Sell the first tranche at the lowest price tier ("first money in"), which LatAm pre-sale buyers already expect to rise by phase.
  • Set your assignment/flip policy up front: allow assignment within the tranche so a syndicator can place units with its own investors, but cap/fee third-party flips to protect retail pricing.

What to prepare (the bulk pitch pack)

Institutional Offering Memorandum (project, Marriott license terms, Big Key track record, ~$120–148M sell-out, timeline, entitlement status) · bulk price matrix (retail vs. tranche price at 20/35/50-unit breaks) · pro forma (10% leaseback yrs 1–2 as cash-on-cash, then Bonvoy program ADR/occupancy/RevPAR → 5–10 yr IRR, Grove as comp) · leaseback & rental-program terms · deposit/escrow + covenant treatment · assignment policy · cross-border financing pack.

The private investor-dinner playbook (highest conversion)

  • Format: weeknight, 2.5–3 hrs, private dining room of a 5-star hotel (Four Seasons / St. Regis / JW Marriott — on-brand with Bonvoy). Cocktails → seated dinner → 10–12 min AMBAR deck → numbers + Q&A → limited-allocation close with on-site contracts and reservation desk.
  • Size: 20–40 pre-qualified attendees (rank by buying intent before the event).
  • Source attendees via: partner brokers (co-host, referral fee), private banks/wealth advisors, existing-owner referrals ("bring a peer"), and EB-5/E-2 migration attorneys. Best move: host the dinner the same week as a relevant expo and skim the highest-intent visitors.
  • Budget: ~$150–400/head; ~$20–40K all-in per city stop (venue, A/V, materials, travel, broker fees).
  • Expect: ~3–6 reservations per well-qualified 30-person dinner. A 5–6 city roadshow ≈ 15–30+ reservations — the bulk of this channel's target.

07 — Cross-Cutting Enabler · The Closing Desk

None of the channels close without this.

Every channel dies at the same friction points: "how do I pay for it?" and "how does a foreigner actually close?" Build one cross-border closing desk that serves all four channels.

The binding constraint: AMBAR is a condo-hotel

Condotels are a narrow financing niche. Realistically model ~35–40% down and ~7–8% rates for financed units, and route buyers to multi-program brokers who can match condo-hotel eligibility lender-by-lender: America Mortgages (strong LatAm desk), HomeAbroad, Bennett Capital and Foundation Mortgage (Miami; condotel experience). Many LatAm buyers will simply pay cash — which skips this entirely and closes faster.

Long-lead items — start at contract, not closing

  • ITIN (tax ID for buyers without SSN): 5 weeks–3 months. The most common timeline killer — start day one.
  • Property-specific Power of Attorney, notarized + apostilled, for remote closing (FL allows RON / mail-away). Title insurers reject overly broad POAs — have a FL attorney draft it per-transaction.
  • Lender condo-hotel approval — confirmed unit-by-unit.

Moving the money (the likeliest date-slip)

  • Mexico — frictionless; bank wires unrestricted. Fastest closers.
  • Colombia — legal but documentation-heavy (regulated FX market); keep clean paperwork from day one for later repatriation.
  • Brazil — central-bank-mediated; the IOF tax code must be correct (~0.38% benchmark; wrong code = much higher). Add days–weeks.
  • Argentina — controls largely lifted April 2025 but volatile (partial re-tightening Sept 2025). Build buffer; confirm rules at transfer time.

Buyer-confidence talking points

Escrow (FL Statute §718.202): the first up to 10% of price sits in protected escrow; deposits above 10% may fund construction if the contract provides — a concrete trust point for pre-construction buyers. FIRPTA is not a purchase barrier — buyers purchase from a US developer, so withholding only matters on their future resale; don't let it scare anyone off.

This is research for sales enablement, not legal or tax advice — buyers should engage a FL real estate attorney and cross-border tax counsel. Rates/terms are ballparks to verify per file.
